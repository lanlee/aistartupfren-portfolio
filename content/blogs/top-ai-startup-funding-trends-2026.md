## Introduction

If you're raising capital for an AI startup right now, you're navigating a market that looks nothing like it did even 12 months ago.

Through the first half of 2026, AI companies raised **$399 billion** globally. For perspective, that's nearly double the $215.9 billion raised in all of 2025, and it puts the market on pace for roughly **$798 billion** by year-end — a 270% increase in a single year.

And yet, deal count dropped 17%.

That's the defining tension of AI startup funding in 2026: **more capital than ever, but flowing through fewer companies with far sharper criteria.** The hype-era "raise on a deck and a dream" window is closed. What replaced it is more concentrated, more demanding, and — for founders who understand the new rules — still remarkably opportunity-rich.

Here are the trends shaping AI startup funding right now, and what they mean for founders.

---

## The AI Power Law Era Has Arrived

### Three Mega-Rounds Dominated Q1

The first quarter of 2026 was, by any measure, unprecedented. Q1 alone saw **$267.2 billion** invested into VC-backed companies — that's 83% of all 2025's total, in a single quarter. The engine, overwhelmingly, was AI.

Consider this: **three deals** — OpenAI ($122B in March), Anthropic ($30B in February, followed by $65B in May), and xAI ($20B in January) — accounted for **65% of all U.S. venture dollars** invested in Q1. AI now commands **89%** of all U.S. venture deal value, a figure that would have seemed absurd just a few years ago.

For context, Mayfield's Navin Chaddha, a three-decade VC veteran, said he had "never seen a single quarter like Q1 2026."

### What This Means for Founders

The power law is now the defining force in AI venture capital. Frontier labs absorb the vast majority of dollars, and most other startups compete for what remains. But "what remains" is still enormous in absolute terms — the tail of the distribution is just changing shape.

The practical implication: **investor attention is scarce.** Standing out requires more than a clever demo. It requires a clear thesis on why your company survives — and thrives — in a world where the frontier labs keep getting bigger.

## Scaleup Concentration: $100M+ Rounds Eat the World

Stage data from Dealroom tells a stark story. Over the trailing four quarters through mid-2026:

| Stage | Share of Capital Raised |
|---|---|
| Scaleup ($100M+ rounds) | 89% |
| Breakout ($15M–$100M) | 8% |
| Startup (under $15M) | 3% |

The number of rounds exceeding **$100 million surged 77% year-over-year to 738 deals**, capturing roughly 65% of all venture funding globally.

Notable scaleup rounds from recent months tell the story:

- **OpenAI** — $122B (March 2026)
- **Anthropic** — $65B Series H (May 2026)
- **xAI** — $20B Series E (January 2026)
- **Waymo** — $16B (February 2026)
- **Prometheus** — $12B Growth Equity (June 2026)
- **DeepSeek** — $7.4B Early VC (June 2026)

### The Two-Track Market

What's emerging is a bifurcated landscape. On one track: a handful of companies raising rounds at valuations that rival public-market giants. On the other: thousands of earlier-stage startups raising smaller rounds with dramatically higher bars for proof.

PitchBook calls this "fewer deals, bigger bets." If you're not in the elite tier, you need to show **efficient growth and real revenue conversion** — not just ambition.

## The Three Layers of AI Investing

Investor interest is distributing across three distinct tiers, each with its own capital dynamics:

### 1. Foundation Model Labs

This is where the headline numbers live. Training a frontier model now costs **$100 million to $1 billion** per generation in compute alone. Nearly every $10B+ AI round sits here. The barrier to entry is astronomical, and the field is consolidating to a small number of global players: OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Google DeepMind, and emerging contenders like DeepSeek in China and Mistral in Europe.

**For founders:** You almost certainly are not building a frontier model company. That's fine. The opportunity is in layers 2 and 3.

### 2. AI Infrastructure

Datacenters, proprietary silicon, GPU supply chains, energy infrastructure. This layer is seeing explosive growth as the compute bottleneck becomes the most pressing constraint in the AI ecosystem. AI computing infrastructure VC grew **+329% over two years**, reaching $12.7 billion in 2025.

Key data points:
- Big Tech's 2026 AI infrastructure spending has surpassed **$635 billion**
- The combined CapEx of the top eight cloud service providers is on track to exceed **$710 billion**
- KKR is preparing a **$10 billion+ AI infrastructure venture** led by former AWS chief Adam Selipsky
- U.S. power equipment spending for data centers is forecast to triple to ~$65 billion by 2030

### 3. AI-Native Applications

This is where **most new AI unicorns are being minted.** Rounds are smaller ($10 million to $500 million), but deal volume is much higher. The prize here is distribution, workflow lock-in, and proprietary data — not raw model capability.

## Geographic Gravity: The Bay Area Effect

AI is pulling capital, talent, and entire ecosystems back into dense geographic hubs — and the Bay Area is the epicenter.

In the trailing 12 months through Q2 2026:
- **Bay Area** — $362.9B in AI VC
- **London** — $16.2B
- **New York City** — $15.9B
- **Los Angeles** — $9.7B
- **Beijing** — $7.1B
- **Shanghai** — $5.8B

The Bay Area alone captured **83% of every VC dollar** in Q1 2026. It hosts **4,529 VC-backed AI startups** and **256 unicorns**.

But the fastest growth is happening elsewhere. Top hubs by VC growth rate (LTM vs 2025):
1. **Barcelona** — +414%
2. **Mumbai** — +405%
3. **Shanghai** — +330%

### Europe's AI Funding Surge

European AI is having a breakout year. Roughly half of all European venture funding in 2026 has gone to AI-related companies. Quarterly venture funding exceeded **$17 billion** in both Q4 2025 and Q1 2026, up a third year-over-year.

Three new AI labs spun out of DeepMind alone — collectively raising **$2.6 billion** in 2026. Notion Capital reports **81% of early-stage European companies** (pre-Series A) are now AI-native, up from 50% a year prior.

Mistral (France) has raised $4 billion to date. In April 2026, Germany's Aleph Alpha merged with Canada's Cohere at a **$20 billion valuation**.

### Agentic AI Goes Mainstream

AI agents saw **$2.9 billion in funding across 50+ deals**, with agents growing +361% over two years. Notable deals include Lyzr raising $100M using its own AI agent to help pitch investors, a16z leading a $20M seed round for agent infrastructure startup Runta, and Nous Research closing $75M at a $1.5B valuation.

What investors want to see: agents moving from "cool demo" to **budgeted enterprise spend.** Only about one in 10 companies have scaled AI agents from experimentation to production — that gap represents both risk and opportunity.

### Physical AI & Robotics

The physical world is having its AI moment. There were **17 humanoid robot deals** in Q1 2026 alone. European startup Humanoid raised $152M at a $1.35B valuation to become Europe's first pure-play humanoid robotics unicorn. Genesis AI is in talks to raise ~$500M for robot "brains."

### Vertical AI in Regulated Industries

AI startups targeting specific industries — legal, healthcare, finance, compliance — are seeing strong investor appetite. Examples include Norm AI raising $120M at a $1.2B valuation for legal and regulatory AI, Gradient Labs raising $26M for autonomous compliance agents in banking, and NeuralTrust raising a $20M seed (Europe's largest cybersecurity seed) to govern enterprise AI agents.

**Why investors like vertical AI:** Proprietary data, regulatory moats, and workflow depth make switching costs high — the exact attributes that CRV says separate durable companies from thin wrappers.

### AI Safety & Security

AI safety is the fastest-growing sub-sector at **+708% two-year growth**. While the absolute numbers ($2.4B in 2025) are small compared to foundation model spending, the growth rate signals a market that's about to scale. Notable rounds include former SentinelOne executives raising $100M for AI security startup Neo, Gray Swan closing $40M for secure AI deployment, and Straiker raising $64M to secure AI agents.

## What Investors Are Looking for in 2026

The bar has risen sharply. CRV's analysis of what closes rounds in 2026 identifies three non-negotiable criteria:

### 1. Team Depth & Domain Credibility

This is the most heavily weighted factor. Technical depth combined with domain expertise is the baseline. Frontier lab backgrounds command a talent premium, but deep industry expertise paired with technical skill carries equivalent weight.

### 2. Durability Beyond the Model

The question every investor asks now: "If a foundation model releases something 10x better tomorrow, does your company still exist?"

The strongest positions come from:
- **Proprietary data** that grows more useful with use
- **Deep workflow integrations** that make migration expensive
- **Vertical expertise** in regulated or complex industries

Advantages built on prompting or current model superiority erode within months.

### 3. Traction That Proves Conversion

The shift from "how many pilots?" to "which pilots converted to budgeted spend?" is one of the most important changes in the 2026 funding landscape. Enterprise funding from operational budgets — not experimental innovation budgets — is the signal investors now require.

### Key Benchmarks to Know
- **Median seed-to-Series-A time** has stretched to **20 months** — do not plan on 12
- **Valuation multiples** remain higher for AI vs non-AI startups, but revenue quality faces much sharper scrutiny
- **Metric stack now includes**: compute economics, revenue per employee, burn multiple, pilot conversion rates, alongside standard CAC/LTV/churn

## What's Cooling Off

Not all AI is hot. Investors are actively avoiding:

- **Thin LLM wrappers and aggregators** — if a customer can switch to a competitor in minutes, there's no moat
- **Horizontal general-purpose AI tools** — CIOs are pushing back on vendor sprawl
- **Companies pitching model performance as a lasting advantage** — the rate of model improvement means today's advantage is tomorrow's table stakes
- **Pilot activity dressed as commercial traction** — investors have learned to look past pilots to actual conversion

---

## Corporate VC: Fewer Bets, Bigger Checks

Corporate venture capital is following the same concentration trend as the broader market. PitchBook reports CVCs are **spending more on fewer deals**, focusing their dollars on strategic AI investments that align with parent-company priorities.

Notable CVC activity:
- Dell Technologies Capital (enterprise AI, infrastructure)
- Samsung in talks to invest in Mistral AI at $29.5B valuation
- KKR's $10B+ AI infrastructure initiative
- Big Tech's $635B+ combined infrastructure spend

For founders, corporate VCs can offer strategic value (distribution, credibility, partnership) but often come with strings around exclusivity, data rights, or acquisition paths.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **The market has bifurcated.** Frontier labs absorb nearly all mega-round capital, but a healthy ecosystem of application-layer, infrastructure, and vertical AI companies continues to raise — with higher proof bars. Know which track you're on and fundraise accordingly.

2. **Durability beats performance.** Investors are skeptical of advantages tied to current model capabilities. Build moats through proprietary data, workflow integration, or regulated-industry expertise — not through being the smartest prompt engineer.

3. **Geography is destiny, but it's shifting.** The Bay Area remains overwhelmingly dominant, but Barcelona, Mumbai, and Shanghai are the fastest-growing AI funding hubs. European AI is having a breakout year, with 81% of pre-Series A startups now AI-native.

4. **Agents, safety, and vertical AI are the hottest sub-sectors.** Agentic AI (+361%), AI safety (+708%), and vertical AI in regulated industries are seeing the strongest growth rates. Physical AI and robotics are also accelerating rapidly.

5. **The seed-to-Series-A timeline has stretched.** Plan for 20 months, not 12. The combination of higher proof bars and investor concentration means earlier-stage companies need more runway and more revenue before their next round.

6. **Prove conversion, not pilots.** Enterprise operational budget spend is the signal that resonates with investors in 2026. Demos and pilots don't close rounds anymore — committed, budgeted deployments do.
